About

I recently completed my MSc in Physics at the University of Milan (110/110 cum laude), with a thesis in the theory of continuously monitored open quantum systems. I study what you can do with the information a continuous measurement gives you: which states it lets you prepare, and how much sharper it makes parameter estimation. The tools come from quantum information, quantum optics and metrology.

I work as much in code as on paper: stochastic simulations in Python and QuTiP, and lower-level work in C++. I came back to research after two years teaching computer science in a high school.

Interests

  • Continuously monitored open quantum systems

    Stochastic master equations, quantum trajectories, and unravellings of the Lindblad dynamics.

  • Quantum metrology

    Quantum Fisher information and measurement-based strategies for enhanced parameter estimation.

  • Quantum state engineering

    Using continuous monitoring and feedback to prepare entangled and spin-squeezed states.

  • Fault-tolerant quantum computing

    An emerging interest in quantum error correction and fault-tolerant quantum algorithms.

Master's thesis

Entangling Unravellings for Quantum State Engineering and Quantum Metrology

Supervisors: Prof. Marco G. Genoni · Dr. Eoin O'Connor — University of Milan

The thesis studies how different continuous-measurement strategies, or unravellings, of an open quantum system's dynamics can generate entanglement and spin squeezing, and quantifies the resulting metrological advantage through the quantum Fisher information.
